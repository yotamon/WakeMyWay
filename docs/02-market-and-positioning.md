# Market and positioning

## Category

Wake My Way sits at the intersection of:

- alarm clock
- behavioral activation
- conversational voice experience
- personalized/adaptive assistant behavior

It should **not** be marketed as a general AI assistant that happens to include alarms.

The category sentence is intentionally simple:

> **A personal alarm that learns how to wake you.**

## Core competitive landscape

### Alarmy

The clearest validation that users will tolerate friction and pay for reliable wake-up behavior.

Strengths:

- large installed base
- strong alarm category recognition
- missions such as math, QR/photo and walking
- strong habit/reliability positioning

Gap for WMW:

Alarmy primarily forces the user to prove wakefulness through a mission. Wake My Way aims to choose the **least aggressive intervention that works for the individual**, with conversation, context and movement used adaptively rather than as generic punishment.

### ClockClanker

The closest conceptual competitor found in discovery. It includes an AI talking alarm, conversation, weather/calendar context, wake style such as Gentle/Firm, First Action, streaks and evening preparation.

Gap for WMW:

"An AI talks to you when the alarm rings" is already reproducible and cannot be the moat. WMW therefore centers the deterministic adaptive wake strategy, activation evidence, reliability degradation, personality behavior and learning loop.

### Matin

Voice morning briefing using calendar/weather and personalities.

Gap:

More passive morning dashboard/briefing. WMW's job is behavioral transition out of bed.

### MorningCall

Phone-call-style wake experience with briefing. The urgency/familiarity of a call is behaviorally interesting.

Gap:

WMW owns a full local alarm runtime rather than relying on call-style delivery as the core reliability mechanism.

### Wake AI / similar AI voice alarms

Validate that customized TTS and generated wake messages are intuitive features.

Gap:

TTS is commodity. WMW's value must come from learning **when and how to intervene**.

### Hatch / Sleep Cycle / Google routines

Adjacent morning/sleep products.

Strengths:

- bedtime ecosystem
- hardware or sleep analytics
- routines and environment integration

Gap:

WMW deliberately avoids becoming a sleep platform. Its job is a narrow transition from chosen wake time to meaningful activation.

## Positioning statement

For people who genuinely intend to get up but repeatedly snooze or return to bed, Wake My Way is an adaptive alarm that learns which combination of conversation, context, movement and friction works for them. Unlike ordinary alarms that simply ring, or mission alarms that apply the same force every morning, WMW learns how to use the least aggressive intervention that reliably gets that individual moving.

## Brand/category message hierarchy

### Brand name

**Wake My Way**

### Primary promise

**Wake up your way.**

### Functional explanation

**An alarm that learns what works for you.**

### Strong product positioning alternatives

- Your alarm knows when. Wake My Way learns how.
- There is no one right way to wake up. There is your way.
- More than a ring. A way to wake.
- An alarm that gets to know morning-you.

## Competitive moat hypothesis

The potential moat is not the voice model or TTS provider.

It is the accumulated relationship between:

```text
wake context
+ strategy used
+ physical/voice signals
+ outcome
+ feedback
= individual wake profile
```

A new alarm can know that the user set 08:00. A mature Wake My Way profile can know that:

- direct prompts work better than generic motivation
- humor works only after first engagement
- movement should begin earlier on Mondays
- calendar urgency improves response on important mornings
- one short snooze helps but multiple snoozes predict failure
- the user often claims to be awake before meaningful movement

## Market risks

1. Competitors can add AI features quickly.
2. Alarm reliability failures destroy trust disproportionately.
3. Voice costs can make unit economics poor if sessions are long.
4. Users may find persistent conversation annoying if adaptation is weak.
5. OS policies around alarms/background behavior can change.

The strategy is therefore to make reliability, behavioral adaptation, low annoyance and local fallbacks core product capabilities rather than secondary polish.
